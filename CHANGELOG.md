## [0.1.0] - 2026-11-08

### Added

- The mapping grammar, serialisable to JSON: `TimeMap` (`audio = offset_s + real / speedup`, its inverse, and spans and frequencies through rhythm-units' `scale_period` and `scale_frequency`); `ParamMap` (`constant`, `linear` and `exponential`, each optionally clamped) for gain in dB, pan and note length; `PitchMap` (`hz`, `midi` with an optional `Scale`, `rhythm` through rhythm-units and pitch-math's `octave_transpose`, `fold` through pitch-math's `octave_fold`); `Scale` with `quantise` (nearest scale note, halves up) and `major_pentatonic`; and `Mapping`, which records every constant.
- `sonify`, which applies a mapping to a data series, one `Event` per point; `render`, which plays events with synthesis-theory's six instruments (`Instrument`), panned and mixed by signal-core with gains from audio-safety-math's `db_to_amplitude`; `render_wav`; and `sha256_hex`.
- `Mapping::check`, which checks every constant of a mapping on its own, and the output of every constant map, before there is any data (a `fold`'s `low_hz` within pitch-math's range, an octave count that some frequency heard at the rhythm's speed-up survives, and a constant gain whose amplitude is finite included; `sonify` runs it, so a mapping is checked even for a series with no points), and `Instrument::check`. Whole numbers in JSON (`octaves`, `root`, `pitch_classes`) may be written as whole floats.
- `audify`, a port of `lifesound.audify` with two changes: audio that would not vary is an error (a constant series, silence included, or interpolated samples that are all equal), and the mean is removed from the samples less the first one, so a series that varies by a unit in the last place keeps that variation instead of gaining a large offset (a sample at a data point's own time takes its value, as in `numpy.interp`); and `heard_hz`, the frequency `k / T` of a rhythm of period `T` at speed-up `k`.
- `write_manifest` and `Manifest`: the draft format `sonification-manifest/0.1-draft` (title, creator, the audio's licence, software, sources, every mapping and event, render settings, and the WAV file's frames, peak and SHA-256), with `to_json`, `from_json` and `reproduces_events` (whether a manifest describes its own events: they reproduce from its mappings, its claims are among its sources, its instrument list is the events' own, its audio is `wav-pcm16` in 2 channels, and its title, creator, licence and sources have the form `write_manifest` requires), and its JSON Schema 2020-12 in `schema/manifest.schema.json`, whose text patterns list the blank characters (control characters and white space) by code point and end in `(?![\s\S])`, the end of the text in ECMA-262 and Python's `re` alike. Titles, creators and licences must not be blank or hold control characters, and claim ids and DOIs hold no blank characters. Seeds are at most `MAX_SEED` (2⁵³ − 1) in every language, so JavaScript reads them exactly.
- `detect_onsets`, an energy-based onset detector whose window energies are raised to a −90 dB power floor (`OnsetParams::floor_db`) if below it, set so that 16-bit dither is never an onset; `verify`, which matches the onsets to a manifest's events, recovers the real times, checks the manifest's description of its events and the audio's length, and reports per-event errors and missing, extra and within-note onsets; and `verify_wav`, which does the same for a WAV file and checks that the manifest's events render to exactly that file, with the manifest's peak.
- `SonifyError` for invalid input (a note's gain whose amplitude is not finite included, in `sonify` as in `render`, named `gain_db`), naming the argument it is about and keeping the names and messages of errors from rhythm-units, pitch-math, signal-core and synthesis-theory.
- Python (PyO3, dicts and numpy arrays) and WebAssembly (plain objects and typed arrays) bindings with the same function names.
- Lean 4 proofs of four P-level claims, reusing the Lean libraries of rhythm-units and pitch-math: the time map is strictly increasing, keeps ratios of durations and inverts; octave shifts keep ratios and cents; the linear map's ends, monotonicity and clamping; the exponential map's ends, monotonicity and constant step ratio. One R claim on the onset-detection approach (Bello et al. 2005).
- Golden vectors (138 cases) shared by Rust, Python and WebAssembly: 73 whose expected outputs come from the oracle (`lifesound.py`'s code, exact Fractions and mpmath, and independent Python versions of the new parts, including the SHA-256 of rendered WAV files and the check of a WAV file) and 65 invalid inputs; property tests of the round trip on float renders and 16-bit WAV files for every instrument at four sample rates, for loud and quiet notes and for overlapping notes (also in seeded random pieces), of what a pass rules out (shifted times, other pitches, false metadata, extra audio, unlisted notes), of determinism, of JSON round trips and of written manifests against the schema, also with unusual characters in their text.
